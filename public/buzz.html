GroupWisdom
Buzz integration

Put GroupWisdom in your Buzz channels

Everything your team posts becomes shared context. When separate people turn out to be circling the same thing, GroupWisdom says so — in the channel, while it still matters.

Setting it up

Two clicks in Buzz, one paste here. Nothing to install, and nothing keeps running on your machine.

1
Copy an invite link from Buzz

In Buzz, click your community's name at the top, choose Invite to community, then Copy link.

Your community name→Invite to community→Copy link
It's the same invite you'd send a person. If you don't see that option, you're not an admin of the community — ask whoever is.
2
Paste it here

That's it — the link says which community to join, so there's nothing else to fill in.

GroupWisdom joins as a normal member — visible in your member list, removable like anyone else.
Other ways to connect

Add it by identity. If your Buzz lets you add a member by public key, this is GroupWisdom's:

loading…

Authorise it without it joining. It then works on your permission and never appears in your member list. Your Buzz secret key signs locally and is never sent anywhere:

npm run buzz:authorize -- <your-nsec> …

Paste the attestation and your community address, then press Connect above.

What it reads

By default it reads every channel it can see. Once connected, each one appears above with a tick box — untick anything you'd rather it stayed out of.

Worth doing deliberately: every message it reads is analysed, and analysis costs money. Pointing it at the two or three channels where real work happens costs a few dollars a month. Pointing it at a busy company-wide workspace costs considerably more, for wisdom you probably didn't want about lunch plans.

It never reads direct messages, and never a channel it hasn't been added to.

What comes back

Wisdom is posted into the channel as an ordinary message from Wisdom Agent. It only speaks when separate contributions add up to something none of them said alone:

Wisdom Agent
One-tap payment is ready for rollout
Sarah's redesign tested 22% faster, James's interviews show a clear preference, and Priya's pilot halved support tickets — three independent angles on the same conclusion.
→ Sarah has already validated the speed gain, so you can build the rollout case from her numbers rather than re-running it.

That last line is the point of the whole thing: it hands you a teammate's finished work instead of assigning you more.
