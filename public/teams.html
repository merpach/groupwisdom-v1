GroupWisdom for Teams
Microsoft Teams

Connect your team

Add GroupWisdom to a team, enter the code it posts, and choose which project the team belongs to. Until you do, it is not reading anything.

1
Get it into your Teams

GroupWisdom is one small app package. There are three ways in, and the first one works in every organisation.

Download groupwisdom-teams.zip

Send the zip to whoever runs your Microsoft 365

Teams admin center › Manage apps ›Upload new app

One upload, no approval step. A few hours later it appears under Built for your org in the Teams store, and from then on anyone can open the store, find GroupWisdom, and choose Add to a team. Nobody needs custom-app permissions, and this route cannot be switched off.


      
    


    
Or submit it from Teams yourself

    

      Teams›Apps›
      Manage your apps›Upload an app
    

    
Choose the option to submit it to your organisation. Any user can; an admin approves it, and it appears under Built for your org the same way.


    
Or, if you run your own Microsoft 365, upload it directly

    

      Teams›Apps›
      Manage your apps›Upload an app›
      Upload a custom app
    

    
Pick the zip and choose Add to a team. Microsoft's own steps, if you want them with screenshots. If "Upload a custom app" is missing, custom uploads are switched off for the organisation — an admin can turn them on under Setup policies, or just use the first route; it never is.


    
Whichever way it arrives, GroupWisdom joins the team, reads only that team's standard channels, and posts a message with a Connect this team button. Press it and the code below fills itself in.

Connected

    

      GroupWisdom now reads the channels of this team and speaks when two separate pieces of work add up to
      something neither said alone. Most of the time it says nothing — that is the intended result.
    

    

      To quiet it: say @GroupWisdom mute in a channel, or mute today for the rest
      of the day. It keeps reading and still answers when asked. Removing the app from the team stops it entirely.
    

    

      
TeamProjectChannels

      
    

  


  
What it reads

  

    Message text, the name of the person who wrote it, and the channel it came from. Nothing from private channels,
    and nothing from chats it has not been added to. What it says in one channel is built only from what that
    channel can see.
  

  

    Raw message text is deleted after 30 days. What is kept beyond that is a bounded summary of what the team has
    established. Nothing read here trains any model. The full detail is in the
    privacy policy.
