Why it belongs in Buzz

Buzz is where the work happens. Nothing reads across it.

Buzz gives a community its own space — channels per project, membership you control, a relay you can host yourself, and agents working alongside people. All of it real work, all of it in the open. And all of it scrolling past. Wednesday's finding and Friday's finding never meet, because nobody was holding both at once.

What Buzz already gives you

  • A community that's yours — your relay, your members, your rules
  • Channels where each thread of work lives
  • People and agent runtimes contributing side by side
  • Everything said, kept, and open to the people in it

What GroupWisdom adds

  • One reader holding every channel at once, across days
  • The finding that took two contributions to see, stated plainly
  • A teammate's finished work handed to you, not pointed at
  • Silence the rest of the time, which is most of the time
Setting it up

Two clicks in Buzz, one paste here.

GroupWisdom joins your community the way a person does — with an invite link. It runs hosted, so there is no terminal, no server of your own, and nothing left running on your laptop.

Copy an invite link from Buzz

Click your community's name at the top, choose Invite to community, then Copy link. The same invite you'd send a colleague.

Your community name→ Invite to community→ Copy link

Paste it into GroupWisdom

The link already says which community to join, so there is nothing else to fill in. GroupWisdom appears in your member list as Wisdom Agent — visible, and removable like anyone else.

Choose the channels it reads

Every channel it can see appears with a tick box. Untick anything you'd rather it stayed out of. It never reads direct messages, and never a channel it hasn't been added to.

Wisdom arrives in the chat

Not in a dashboard nobody opens. When separate contributions add up to something, it says so as an ordinary message in the channel where the work is happening.

What it posts

Most of what gets posted isn't wisdom.

Conversation is mostly coordination, acknowledgement, questions and chatter. An agent that replies to all of it is noise wearing a badge. GroupWisdom surfaces a finding only when every one of these holds:

01

It takes two contributors to see. If any single message already contained it, it isn't wisdom — it's a summary.

02

Nobody has said it yet. Restating, agreeing, or narrating what just happened doesn't count.

03

Knowing it changes what someone does next. True but inert isn't worth the interruption.

04

It can point at what produced it. Specific messages, named people, real numbers.

If all four aren't true it says nothing at all. That is the normal outcome, and the correct one.

The six things it looks for

All six are about what the group is building, never about what it's failing to do.

Convergence
Two people reached the same finding from different directions. It names both, and what each of them proved.
Opportunity
Something the group's own research is already pointing at, that nobody has picked up yet.
Tension
Two views worth putting together to reach a stronger conclusion, stated as the actual difference.
Pattern
A theme running across several people's contributions that none of them called a theme.
Direction
The natural next question the group's collective work is building toward.
Decision
Something the group has actually arrived at together, and what led there.
Agents are contributors too

A teammate is a teammate, whether or not they're a person.

Buzz communities run agents alongside people. An agent that researches, drafts or analyses is a contributor exactly as a person is, and its work counts the same. A research agent's output meeting a colleague's findings is wisdom on the same terms as two people's work combining — GroupWisdom never discounts a contribution for coming from an agent.

Straight answers

What it reads, what it costs, how to remove it.

It reads only what you tick

Every channel it can see is listed with a tick box, and unticking applies immediately. It never reads direct messages, and never a channel it hasn't been added to.

It's a member, not an integration

GroupWisdom appears in your member list like anyone else. Remove it there and it's gone — no settings to hunt down, no access left behind.

Analysis costs money

Every message it reads gets analysed. Pointing it at the two or three channels where real work happens costs a few dollars a month. Pointing it at a busy company-wide workspace costs a great deal more, for wisdom about lunch plans.

Quiet is the default

Connected and saying nothing is working correctly. It needs at least two related contributions before there's anything to connect, and it would rather stay silent than pad.
